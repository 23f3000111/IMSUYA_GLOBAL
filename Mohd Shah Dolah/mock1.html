— 04 · Frequently asked

Questions, answered.

A short directory of the questions clients most often ask before they pick up the phone — from agent fees and the BOVAEP register, to what an agent actually does for a buyer or a seller in Malaysia.

Ask Shah directly
01 What is a real estate agent? +
A real estate agent is a licensed professional who arranges property transactions — putting buyers and sellers together and acting as their representative in negotiations. Real estate agents in Malaysia are usually compensated by an agency fee, a percentage of the property's purchase price, so their income depends entirely on their ability to close the deal in the client's interest.
02 What are the responsibilities of a real estate agent? +
Real estate agents assist clients in buying, selling and renting properties. A real estate agent (or sales person) does similar work to a real estate agency, but only licensed agencies (E-license) are permitted to manage their own real estate businesses. Individual agents must operate under a registered firm — in my case, Landsworth Properties Sdn Bhd, E(1)1959.
03 What does a real estate agent do for a buyer? +
When helping you buy a home, the agent's role is to work within your best interests — provide and gather information on neighbourhoods and homes, help you avoid potential pitfalls (Bumi conditions, encumbrances, master-title issues), write and negotiate an offer on your behalf, and guide you through the buying process all the way until you get the keys.
04 What does a real estate agent do for a property owner? +
For an owner, the agent prices the property fairly, markets it, screens enquiries, conducts viewings, negotiates with buyers and coordinates with bank panels, valuers and lawyers through to the closing. The goal is the best price within a reasonable time — not the fastest sale at any price.
05 How much is real estate agent fees? +
For a subsale transaction the fee is a maximum of 3% of the property's sale price. For a rental, the fee is 1.25 month from the rental amount (varying by tenancy term). Both fees are before the additional 8% SST. These rates are gazetted under the Valuers, Appraisers, Estate Agents & Property Managers Act 1981 (Act 242), Seventh Schedule, Rule 48.
06 Who regulates the real estate industry in Malaysia? +
BOVAEP — also known as the Lembaga Penilai, Pentaksir, Ejen Harta Tanah & Pengurus Harta (LPPEH) — is the body tasked with regulating Valuers, Appraisers, Estate Agents and Property Managers in Malaysia. Set up in 1981, BOVAEP falls under the purview of the Ministry of Finance, with its operation governed by the Valuers, Appraisers, Estate Agents and Property Managers Act 1981.
07 How can I check if an agent is registered with BOVAEP? +
You can verify any registered agent on the BOVAEP public register at lppeh.gov.my. Look up the agent's PEA number (for probationary estate agents) or REA number (for registered estate agents). My PEA number is PEA 3211, operating under Landsworth Properties Sdn Bhd E(1)1959.
08 What other terms in Bahasa represent "Estate Agent"? +
In Bahasa Malaysia, the same role goes by Ejen Hartanah or Ejen Harta Tanah. Informally you'll also hear broker hartanah. The official, gazetted term used by the regulator is Ejen Harta Tanah, which is why BOVAEP's full name is Lembaga Penilai, Pentaksir, Ejen Harta Tanah & Pengurus Harta.
09 Why brand yourself as a "Property Agent KL"? +
Because that's how my clients actually search. Buyers and sellers in Klang Valley type variations like property agent KL, real estate agent Kuala Lumpur, ejen hartanah KL — and the truth is, I do work primarily across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and the Cheras–Setiawangsa corridor. The labels are different; the licence is the same.
10 Are you registered with BOVAEP? +
Yes. Registered as PEA 3211 with the Board of Valuers, Appraisers, Estate Agents and Property Managers Malaysia (BOVAEP / LPPEH). Practising under Landsworth Properties Sdn Bhd, E(1)1959. Verified member of MIEA and MIPEAC.
