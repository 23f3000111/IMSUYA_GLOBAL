05 · Frequently asked

Questions,
answered.

A short directory of the questions clients most often ask — from agent fees and the BOVAEP register, to what an agent actually does for a buyer or seller in Malaysia.

Ask Shah directly
01 What is a real estate agent? +
A real estate agent is a licensed professional who arranges property transactions — putting buyers and sellers together and acting as their representative in negotiations. In Malaysia, agents are compensated by an agency fee, a percentage of the property's purchase price.
02 How much does an agent cost? +
For a sale, the standard co-broke fee in Malaysia is 2–3% of the transaction price, paid by the vendor. For rental, it's typically one month's rent for a one-year lease. As a buyer, you don't normally pay the agent directly.
03 Why use a licensed agent over a negotiator? +
A licensed agent (PEA or REA) is personally registered with BOVAEP and accountable under the Valuers, Appraisers, Estate Agents and Property Managers Act 1981. With Shah, the person who answers is the same one who holds the license.
04 What is Freehold vs Leasehold in Malaysia? +
Freehold means ownership of the land indefinitely. Leasehold means the land is held for a fixed period — commonly 99 years — and must be renewed, which can affect resale value and financing options. Shah verifies the actual title before listing.
05 What does a Bumi condition mean? +
Some properties have a Bumiputera condition attached to the title, restricting sale or transfer to Bumiputera purchasers only. This affects the eligible buyer pool and can influence market value. Shah identifies this at listing stage so there are no surprises.
